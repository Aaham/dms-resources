7 questions

Board and executive AI brief

Seven questions for leadership to answer before approving a new use of AI. Fill in the answers and print a one-page brief for your board or executive team.

What you type is saved in this browser. It is not sent to DMS.

The proposal

Write one brief for each use. “AI for the whole organization” is too broad to decide on.

1. What problem are we solving?

How often the work comes up, and what it costs now in time, money, delays or errors.

Something you can measure, and why a tool is needed to get there.

2. What information will the tool receive?

Describe the kinds of information, such as intake notes or donor records. Don’t type the information itself.

Public is already published. Internal is not for release. Sensitive is about clients, staff, donors, finances, health, immigration or location. Regulated is covered by a law, a grant, a contract or a professional duty.

For example: HIPAA, a grant agreement, a professional duty of confidentiality, your privacy notice.

3. Who could be affected if it is wrong?

Think of clients, applicants, staff, donors, partners and the public. Name groups, not individuals.

4. What remains a human decision?

For example: draft, summarize, sort or recommend.

Someone who knows the work, has the time to check it and can reject it.

5. What has the vendor committed to?

Cover whether your information trains a model, how long it is kept, how it is deleted, security, notice of incidents, the other companies that receive it and changes to the terms. The AI vendor questions ask about each.

6. How will we know whether it works?

Include the time or money saved, the quality of the work, and whether the people affected are treated fairly and still trust it.

7. Who can stop it?

The leader who can fund the use, change it or stop it.

Decision

To record the decision at the meeting, leave this part blank and print the brief.
